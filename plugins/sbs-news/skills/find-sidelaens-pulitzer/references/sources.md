# Danish source seeds

Use these as starting points for discovery, not as a whitelist. Do not mechanically visit every site on every run. Sample across categories, run broader Danish searches, and follow promising results to other legitimate Danish publishers.

## Established national media

Serious newsrooms can produce especially strong candidates when an authoritative tone, substantial reporting, expert analysis, or conspicuous production is applied to something tiny or self-evident:

- Politiken — https://politiken.dk/
- Information — https://www.information.dk/
- Frihedsbrevet — https://frihedsbrevet.dk/
- Berlingske — https://www.berlingske.dk/
- Jyllands-Posten — https://jyllands-posten.dk/
- DR Nyheder — https://www.dr.dk/nyheder
- TV 2 Nyheder — https://nyheder.tv2.dk/
- Weekendavisen — https://www.weekendavisen.dk/
- Kristeligt Dagblad — https://www.kristeligt-dagblad.dk/

Look for overanalysis of everyday subjects, self-evident conclusions presented as insight, grave framing of minor events, or an unintentionally funny mismatch between headline and reporting. Exclude columns, commentary, and headlines whose humor or irony is deliberate.

## Gossip and celebrity

These often produce small stories, awkward headlines, and disproportionate framing:

- SE og HØR — https://www.seoghoer.dk/
- BILLED-BLADET — https://www.billedbladet.dk/
- Kendte.dk — https://www.kendte.dk/

The mere fact that a story is celebrity gossip is not enough. Look for unintended comedy in the headline, premise, sourcing, or amount of journalistic attention.

## Low-calorie, service, viral, and breaking news

These are useful for tautological headlines, weather non-events, thin explainers, liveblog filler, and ordinary occurrences framed as urgent:

- B.T. — https://www.bt.dk/
- Ekstra Bladet — https://ekstrabladet.dk/
- Dagens.dk — https://www.dagens.dk/
- Nyheder24 — https://nyheder24.dk/
- Avisen.dk — https://www.avisen.dk/
- Presse-fotos.dk — https://presse-fotos.dk/

Search relevant sections and current article pages rather than relying only on front pages. A clickbait headline can qualify, but only when its humor is unintended and the underlying article verifies the premise.

## Search strategy

Prioritize discovery in this order while still sampling every major source group:

1. TV 2 regions, regional/local publishers, and liveblogs
2. Established national media
3. Low-calorie, service, viral, and breaking-news outlets
4. Gossip and celebrity outlets

Directly inspect front pages, latest-news indexes, local subsections, liveblogs, and RSS feeds where available. Mix this with broad and source-specific web searches. Useful discovery angles include recent Danish results involving liveblogs, weather, traffic, animals, celebrities, neighborhood disputes, municipal notices, store openings, queues, puddles, minor mishaps, and reporters sent to inspect ordinary events. These are prompts for exploration, not required subjects.

Treat aggregators, automated rewrite networks, generic AI-content sites, press-release mirrors, municipal announcements, and social reposts as discovery leads only. A final candidate must come from identifiable editorial journalism at the original publisher. If a newsroom merely republishes a press release, it does not qualify unless its own headline, framing, or reporting creates the unintended comedy.

Do not overfit to these domains. Search engines may surface smaller independent city sites, trade publications, free weeklies, or radio sites with a much stronger candidate. Verify that the final item is genuine journalism from its original publisher.
